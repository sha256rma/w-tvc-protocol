# Reference tests catch swaps, not 8-bit copies

As of October 2026, black-box reference tests reliably catch a provider serving a **different model**, catch **4-bit quantization** most of the time, catch **routing dilution** only when the substitute is well separated and the routed share is above roughly 10–40%, and **do not catch an 8-bit or fp8 copy of a 7B-class model** at any published budget. The limit isn't the tests' cleverness. It's the honest null: the same weights served on a different engine, GPU or precision already move outputs more than int8 does, so any test sharp enough to see 8-bit also flags honest providers (IRIS separates 14 of 15 same-model provider pairs, and Ventor-QTest rejects all six third-party DeepSeek routes, including a declared fp8 one). That forces two design choices: thresholds have to be calibrated on a declared set of honest configurations rather than on one reference stack, and a rejection has to be worded as "inconsistent with the declared configuration", not as fraud. Provider-injected system prompts are the other big false-positive source: MET falsely rejects 67% of unchanged models under a short system prompt, while a tool-choice test (AgentProv) drops that to 7%. Cryptographic routes don't close the gap yet. GPU TEEs cost 1–16% and prove which image ran, but NVIDIA's attestation covers firmware and driver, not weights; zkLLM needs about 13 minutes of proving per 13B inference; commit-and-challenge schemes need the provider to opt in. Industry verifiers (Kimi's K2VV, OpenRouter Exacto, Meta's llama-verifications) check tool-call and benchmark contracts and publish unsigned, editable tables. A sealed, committed, timestamped reference with its honest-null band is the piece nobody else publishes, and that's where SPOT fits.

## Different models are easy to catch; same weights at 8-bit aren't

Substitution by a different model is a solved problem at small budgets. LLMmap identifies 42 model versions at **95.3% from 8 active queries** ([LLMmap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15847)), and on a controlled shadow-API testbed it decided honest vs substituted endpoints with 96% accuracy, 3% FPR and 5% FNR ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01919)). Bruckner's single-token battery (one-word answers to prompts like "random number 1–100", 40 cells over 4 languages) reaches **AUC 0.971 and EER 7.3%** over 165 OpenRouter models, and still gets EER under 11% with 8 cells, about a hundred one-token queries ([One Token Is Enough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10252)). IRIS's random-bit-string probes hit substitution AUROC 0.993 from a single response on a six-model Qwen3 ladder ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). AgentProv, MET and RUT each rejected all 630 cross-checkpoint pairs among 36 checkpoints ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)), and KBF's T=0 knowledge-boundary probes flagged 155 of 155 substitutions with 0 of 16 same-model false positives at $0.02–0.24 per audit ([KBF](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29524v2)). The stakes are real: 45.83% of 24 shadow-API endpoints used in research papers failed LLMmap verification, and one Gemini-2.5-flash shadow endpoint scored about 37% on MedQA against 83.82% on the official API ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01919)).

Logit-geometry signatures pin an exact checkpoint from one full logprob vector. The ellipse signature separates Olmo-2 from its own second-to-last checkpoint "by several orders of magnitude" ([Finlayson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14086)). Three limits make it marginal for SPOT: it needs full-vocabulary logprobs, which only about **23% of reachable OpenRouter endpoints** return even in top-k form ([Chauvin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03816)); it checks only the final norm and unembedding, which the authors note "is generally not quantized", so a quantized or fine-tuned body passes; and for open weights the ellipse is public, so anyone holding the weights can produce on-ellipse logprobs ([Finlayson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14086)).

Quantization splits cleanly by bit width. At 7–9B, **8-bit is effectively invisible**: MET's power against int8 Llama-3.1-8B is 0.08 at the default budget ([MET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247)), RUT's power AUC on 8-bit Gemma-2-9B and Mistral-7B is 0.049, with the authors concluding "none of the methods succeed" ([RUT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06975)), text classifiers sit at 49–52% on FP8 and INT8 from 7B to 72B, and MMD power on INT8 8B models never rises above α even under full substitution ([Cai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04715)). AgentProv, MET and RUT all accepted 8-bit Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B served on vLLM ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)). The one exception is model-specific: Llama-3 8B int8 reaches MET power 0.79, and 0.96 in character space once samples rise from 10 to 50 per prompt ([MET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247)). **4-bit is visible**: MET power on nf4 Llama-3.1-8B is 1.00, RUT's 4-bit power AUC is 0.39–0.64 ([MET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247); [RUT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06975)), and IRIS's q4 tell rate on random-bit probes climbs from 0.35 at 0.6B to 1.00 at 4B ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). At 70B the picture flips for 8-bit in controlled local runs: MET int8 power is 1.00 and IRIS's char-n-gram re-analysis separates int8 at AUROC 0.99, yet the same detector gets 0.45 at 8B, and no 70B result isolates precision from serving stack under real provider noise ([MET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247); [IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)).

The reason 8-bit hides is the size of the effect. Weight-only GPTQ W8A16 on Llama2-13b has KL 7.9×10⁻⁵ against 16-bit and flips 0.26–1.05% of MMLU answers, while 4-bit methods flip 4–8% ([Dutta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141)). Red Hat's study puts FP8 at 99.3–100% benchmark recovery at every scale, yet greedy text differs a lot (ROUGE-L about 0.51 at 8B) ([Kurtic et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02355)). So an fp8 copy passes accuracy checks, fails exact-text checks, and lands inside the noise of distribution tests. That would be a quality footnote except for one result: full-precision weights can be tuned so that the fp32 model is benign and its int8, FP4 or NF4 version injects content 66–75% of the time ([Egashira et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18137)). Precision is a security claim, not only a quality one, which argues for references that pin precision alongside the checkpoint hash.

| Threat | Strongest black-box evidence | What still gets through |
|---|---|---|
| Different model or family | LLMmap 95.3% / 8 queries; One Token AUC 0.971; IRIS AUROC 0.993 at m=1; KBF 155/155 | Close relatives at low routing share; spoofed fingerprints |
| 4-bit quantization (≤9B) | MET nf4 power 1.00; RUT AUC 0.39–0.64; IRIS q4 tell rate up to 1.00 | Some models (AgentProv accepted 4-bit Qwen2.5-7B) |
| 8-bit / fp8 (≤9B) | MET int8 0.79–0.96 on Llama-3 8B only | Almost everything else at 7–9B |
| Routing dilution | IRIS: 5/10/20/40% share in about 64/32/16/8 queries for separating substitutes | Close relatives below about 40%; text MMD below 20% |
| Injected system prompt | RUT power 0.86–0.90 on hidden jailbreak prompts | Benign prompts look identical to substitution |

Dilution follows a simple law when a substitute leaves a visible "tell": IRIS's budget is m* = ln(1/δ)/(εq), so a routed share ε needs queries in proportion to 1/ε, and on commercial models 5%, 10%, 20% and 40% dilution was caught in roughly 64, 32, 16 and 8 queries, with a deployed audit at ε=0.3 reaching power 0.85 at pooled FPR 0.017 ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). Without a separating tell the cost grows as ε⁻², and the low-tell cases are exactly the near-relatives: KBF's hardest same-class pair needs 43% routing for 95% TPR ([KBF](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29524v2)), AgentProv accepted a provider that served the genuine model half the time or more ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)), and text MMD against int8 at 70B rejects reliably only at p ≥ 0.6 and falls to α at p ≤ 0.2 ([Cai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04715)).

Provider system-prompt injection is detectable but breaks the other tests. With weights unchanged, a single whitespace system message already flips MET on 3 of 5 models; across empty, short and ~50-token prompts the false-positive rate is **MET 67%, RUT 53%, adapted LLMmap 20%, AgentProv 7%** ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)). It's common: 44% of 75 OpenRouter endpoints report prompt-token counts that differ from the official chat template, nine by a constant positive offset (+25 tokens on one Llama-3.2-3B endpoint) ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)). A dropped leading space at HF Inference alone pushed MET power to 1.00 against an honest Mistral endpoint ([RUT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06975)). The practical fix is ordering: check the prompt-token count first, and treat text-test rejections on an endpoint with an offset as inconclusive rather than as substitution.

Finally, public probe sets are spoofable. GhostPrint LoRA-tunes a weak model to pass LLMmap at up to 95% and an Idiosyncrasies classifier at up to 96.7% while keeping the weak model's MMLU score, knowing only the query *distribution*, not the exact queries; MET was the hardest target at 23–60% ([GhostPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16100)). Ten ownership-fingerprint schemes fall entirely to adaptive attacks ([Nasery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26598)). Full-distribution comparisons against a reference resist best, and sealed, rotated probes help, but Ventor-QTest's authors are right that "freshness therefore prevents exact precomputation, not semantic probe detection" ([Ventor-QTest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16391)).

## Honest serving moves outputs more than fp8 does

Honest variation comes in two kinds with very different sizes, and conflating them is the commonest mistake in this literature. **Configuration defaults applied before generation are huge.** Five engines serving identical weights on one H100 spread up to **16.6 GSM8K points** (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B: Transformers 78.47, Ollama 61.87), and the causes are mundane: Ollama prepends a BOS token even in raw mode (removing it adds 7.35–8.34 points) and applies a hidden repetition penalty of 1.1 (removing it adds 11.67) ([Pape et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19537)). vLLM 0.8.5 ignores the model's generation_config and defaults to temperature 1.0 with max_tokens 16 ([Masoudian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04714)). Kimi K2 on vLLM 0.11.0 parsed fewer than 20% of tool calls until three template and parser bugs were fixed, after which successful calls rose 4.4× ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-10-28-kimi-k2-accuracy)). The Llama-3.2 template embeds today's date, which produced up to a 37.6% swing in apparent cross-GPU divergence when sweeps crossed midnight ([Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624)).

**Numerical drift is small on scores and large on tokens.** Prefix caching, CUDA graphs, batch size and tie-breaking each move GSM8K by under about 1 point with unpredictable sign ([Pape et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19537)). But exact greedy streams break widely: BF16 vs FP16 on the same GPU diverges on **49–100% of prompts** across six 1–7B models, while code-execution outcomes still agree on 97.6% of HumanEval problems where tokens agree on 26.2% ([Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26621)). Unmitigated BF16 across A100, L40S and H100 gives a different token stream on 31–100% of problems ([Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624)). Real providers of Gemma-2-9B agree with a local greedy baseline under 5% of the time, a divergence "often larger than that induced by quantization" ([Cai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04715)), and across 22 provider-model combinations exact T=0 agreement on creative tasks was 0% ([Chandra & Reddy](https://zenodo.org/records/20320651)). Even the serving stack itself is fingerprintable: engine, attention backend and GPU are identified perfectly from T=0 text ([Wimbauer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29979)).

| Honest change | Effect on benchmark score | Effect on exact tokens or fine statistics |
|---|---|---|
| Engine defaults (BOS, repetition penalty, template, sampling) | Up to 10–17 points; tool calls below 20% | Dominates everything else |
| Engine kernels, same config | 0.09–5.7 points in most cells | vLLM diverges from HF on Qwen2.5-1.5B, effect size 0.38 |
| Prefix caching, CUDA graphs, batch size | Under about 1 point each, random sign | Bitwise logits match on only 3/14 prefix-reuse pairs (vLLM default) |
| BF16 vs FP16 | About 1 point | 49–100% of greedy streams differ |
| GPU architecture | Up to 9% accuracy (with batch size and GPU count) | 31–100% of problems differ on some GPU pair |
| GPU swap, distribution test | n/a | RUT rejects 6–9%, MMD 8–14% at α=0.05 |

Sources: [Pape et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19537), [Masoudian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04714), [Vosti](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38981), [Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26621), [Yuan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09501), [Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624), [RUT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06975).

Deterministic serving modes fix the reference side, not the provider side. Thinking Machines' batch-invariant kernels turned 80 distinct completions out of 1,000 T=0 runs on Qwen3-235B into one, tracing the cause to load-dependent batch size ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). vLLM (`VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`) and SGLang (`--enable-deterministic-inference`) ship this, scoped to batch size and request order on one stack ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/); [SGLang docs](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/deterministic_inference.html)). It isn't complete: Vosti's bitwise tests pass vLLM's invariant mode on Llama-3.1-8B but find Gemma3-4B matching on only 0 of 90 chunk-size variations and 5 of 384 prefill-vs-decode pairs ([Vosti](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38981)). Cost estimates range from a 5–8% throughput hit (SPOT's own round 1 on an L4) to a third-party 54–67% loss on an RTX 4090 with CUDA graphs on ([AshrithaG/batch-invariance](https://github.com/AshrithaG/batch-invariance)). Nobody claims cross-GPU, cross-precision or cross-TP bitwise equality in production engines; the best result makes linear layers bitwise identical across Ampere, Ada and Hopper with fixed-order FP32 GEMMs, with attention still unpinned ([Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25624)). SPOT's round 1 fits this picture: on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, one L4 and vLLM 0.30.0, default kernels gave the same greedy answer alone and in a 32-wide batch on 20 of 32 prompts, batch-invariant kernels on 32 of 32. That makes SPOT's reference replayable on its own stack; it does nothing to make a provider's output match.

The consequence for test choice is direct. Exact-match and logprob-trace tests across stacks are unusable. Aggregate scores survive numerical drift but not unpinned defaults. Distribution tests on low-entropy answer sets (one-word answers, tool choice, task outcomes) have the smallest honest nulls, so choosing the statistic matters as much as calibrating it.

## Thresholds belong to a declared honest set, not one stack

The measured honest nulls are well above zero and differ by test. Swapping an A6000 reference for an A100 running the same model makes RUT reject at 5.8–9.4% and MMD at 8.4–14.2% at nominal α=0.05 ([RUT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06975)). One Token's split-half floor within a deployment is JSD 0.140, the cross-provider median for the same model is 0.227, the impostor median is 0.463, and **29% of same-model provider pairs** land beyond the impostor 5th percentile; using a reference from a different provider drops AUC from 0.971 to 0.880 ([One Token Is Enough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10252)). IRIS distinguishes 14 of 15 same-model provider pairs, including two providers that both declare fp8 mistral-nemo at AUROC up to 1.00, "a kernel- rather than precision-level difference"; the only indistinguishable pair was bf16 vs fp16 Llama-3.3-70B ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). Ventor-QTest's official-API self-check centres on zero (S_r = −0.0007, Holm p = 0.515) while all six third-party routes reject, and its authors decline to read that as a changed checkpoint ([Ventor-QTest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16391)). A test calibrated only on the auditor's own stack will therefore flag most honest third parties.

The literature offers a short toolkit for setting thresholds against that null. MET's **intersection-union rule** treats the null as composite (the endpoint equals the fp32 reference *or* the fp16 one) and rejects only if both two-sample tests reject; FPR stays at or below α for any endpoint matching some member, at a cost in power ([MET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20247)). IRIS adds **disjoint calibration splits** (threshold at the 1−α quantile of one split, honest rate re-measured on a second, audit on a third) and an **exact binomial test against a Clopper–Pearson upper bound**; that change cut the 95th-percentile per-pair honest FPR from 0.090 to 0.018 and the pooled mean to 0.0065, costing 7 points of power at ε=0.3 ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). IRIS also shows a fixed-budget test's realized FPR drifting from 0.05 to 0.12–0.18 as m grows from 4 to 32, so thresholds must be recalibrated per budget ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860)). AgentProv permutes endpoint labels within templates so Type-I control holds at any K and N ([AgentProv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00052)); Ventor-QTest subtracts a per-context bias baseline because "finite-sample posterior uncertainty produces a positive baseline even under the null", then applies Holm across routes ([Ventor-QTest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16391)). For continuous monitoring, a betting e-process stopped in about 28–68 queries with honest FPR 0.0045, against 0.0235 for SPRT ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20860); [Richter et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19406)).

Two gaps matter. No auditing paper applies formal TOST equivalence bounds or conformal calibration over a set of honest configurations, and no paper has measured one statistic across the full engine × GPU × TP × precision matrix. SPOT will have to build that matrix itself, which is cheap at 7B and is also the most defensible thing it can publish. The position this evidence supports: use the intersection-union rule over an explicit honest set H, set each member's threshold from a held-out split with an exact upper-bound correction, and treat inclusion in H as a published policy decision. An fp8 provider flagged against an H holding only bf16 and fp16 hasn't been caught cheating; it has been shown to differ from what SPOT accepts as the model.

## Attestation proves what ran; nobody publishes what should have run

Cryptographic and hardware approaches answer a different question, and none answers it cheaply for a public third party yet. **GPU TEEs** are the only deployed option with low overhead: under 7% for most LLM queries on Hopper, with Llama-3.1-8B throughput down 6.85% and 70B essentially unaffected ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03992)), up to 16% TTFT for an 8B model at concurrency 64 in Cai et al.'s measurement ([Cai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04715)), and about 1–3% on a well-configured B200 stack versus 30–40% on stock configurations ([Asad & Grunseid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26575)). But NVIDIA's documented attestation checks driver and VBIOS against reference measurements; it doesn't measure model weights ([NVIDIA docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/attestation/quick-start-guide/latest/attestation-examples/hopper_single_gpu.html)). Weight binding comes only from the operator's measured CPU-TEE container, as in Phala's TDX-plus-H100 deployment on OpenRouter, where per-response signatures use an enclave-generated key ([Phala](https://phala.com/posts/GPU-TEEs-is-Alive-on-OpenRouter)). Trust then rests on NVIDIA and Intel roots plus the operator's published mapping from image to weights.

**Zero-knowledge proofs** of full inference stop at 13B: zkLLM proves a LLaMa-2-13B inference in 803 s on an A100 with a 188 kB proof verified in about 4 s ([zkLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16109)), and practitioners describe zkML as shippable only for small fixed models, while **opML** runs at near-native speed for Llama-2-scale models but rests on at least one honest challenger during a dispute window ([proofoftech.org](https://proofoftech.org/blog/opml-or-zkml/)). The closest cryptographic analogue to SPOT is a Groth16 design that proves a deployment's logits on hidden committed probes stay within a drift bound of the approved model's committed responses, at 1–1.8 s proving time; its authors note "reliability degrades if the provider can learn the probes" ([Wilding et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27954)). **Commit-and-challenge** schemes are cheap but opt-in: CommitLLM commits to weights, quantization, tokenizer, template and decode policy for about 12–14% generation overhead and a CPU-only verifier ([CommitLLM](https://federicocarrone.com/articles/commitllm/)); TopLoc stores 258 bytes per 32 tokens but needs the verifier to recompute with the claimed model ([TOPLOC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16007)); SVIP reaches FPR under 3% but needs a trusted third-party platform ([SVIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22307)).

| Approach | Proves | Cost | Needs provider cooperation | Public, replayable record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Black-box reference tests | Behaviour matches a reference within a noise band | Cents per audit | No | Only if someone publishes it |
| GPU TEE + CPU TEE | This measured image ran on genuine hardware | 1–16% | Yes | Quote is bilateral |
| zkLLM | This exact inference used committed weights (≤13B) | ~13 min per inference | Yes | Proof is public, but no serving path |
| opML | Inference result, unless challenged | Near native | Yes | Depends on challenge game |
| CommitLLM / TopLoc / SVIP | Responses match a private commitment | ~0–14% | Yes | No |
| Industry verifiers (K2VV, Exacto, llama-verifications) | Tool-call and benchmark contracts | 600–4,000 requests | No | Mutable, unsigned tables |

Industry verifiers check contracts rather than identity. Kimi's K2VV sends **4,000 requests per provider**, scores tool-call trigger F1 against the official API, and sets its cutoff (">73%" for K2-thinking, ">80%" for K2-0905) just below the lowest of repeated official runs, which is a sensible honest-null method; its 2025-11-15 tables show providers like Nebius at 50.6% trigger agreement and vLLM at 76% schema accuracy ([K2VV](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/K2-Vendor-Verifier)). The newer Kimi Vendor Verifier adds AIME2025 as a "long-output stress test" for KV-cache and quantization bugs and promises a public leaderboard ([Kimi blog](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-vendor-verifier)). OpenRouter's Auto Exacto routes on tool-call telemetry and reports cutting GLM-4.7 tool-call errors from about 8% to 1%, but holds full data back so providers can "respond before we publish" ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/auto-exacto/)). Meta's llama-verifications expects 100% functional passes and benchmark parity with the model card ([llama-verifications](https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-verifications)). Logprob-based checks such as fallrisk (119/120 correct for $0.72) and Strake (fp quantizations drift about 0.07 nats/token, a 2× cheaper impostor +0.27) are cheapest where logprobs exist ([fallrisk](https://fallrisk.ai/research/endpoint-verification); [Strake](https://strake.dev/blog/detecting-llm-model-substitution)). None of these results is signed, hash-committed or timestamped, none commits to its test set before testing, and K2VV publishes only half its set while openbenchmarks keeps its corpus private ([openbenchmarks](https://github.com/openbenchmarks-labs/inference)). The router-security work makes the same point from the other side, proposing DKIM-style provider-signed response envelopes and noting that no major tool-use API exposes one ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08407)).

## What SPOT should run, threshold and record

### Tests: gate on token counts, add a tool-choice test

A reference test kit built on the strongest published tests (token counts after AgentProv, answer distributions after Bruckner, RUT, MET and random-string probes after IRIS) should make four adjustments. First, make **the token-count check a gate**, not a peer: any endpoint whose prompt-token count differs from the rendered template gets an "injected or altered prompt" finding, and its text-test rejections are reported as inconclusive, since those tests false-alarm on 53–67% of unchanged models under a system prompt. Second, add a **tool-choice test** modelled on AgentProv (it reached 97–100% rejection of cross pairs at 0/36 self-test FPR) because it is the only test measured to survive system-prompt injection; AgentProv's code isn't released, so it has to be reimplemented. A small **T=0 knowledge-boundary test** after KBF is a cheap second survivor (0/30 false positives under role prompts and RAG wrappers). Third, size query budgets from the published power curves rather than by guesswork; for 8-bit, MET needed about five times its default budget (Llama-3 8B int8 went from 0.30 to 0.96). Fourth, **seal and rotate**: draw each audit's prompts fresh from a committed pool, because GhostPrint generalised to held-out queries from a known distribution. Licences matter for a product: RUT and the Berkeley code are MIT and AI-Infra-Guard is Apache-2.0, while MET's repo has no licence and IRIS's is non-commercial, so reimplementations are the right call for a product.

Run the planned **AWQ build of Qwen2.5-7B as a known positive** on every battery. It tells SPOT, on its own GPU, which batteries actually see 4-bit at 7B and at what budget, which is the power number every verdict should cite.

### Thresholds: intersection-union over a published honest set

Build the honest set H on SPOT's own hardware for each model: the batch-invariant bf16 reference (R1b), default kernels (R1a, already collected), fp16 on the T4 (Turing has no bf16, so that run is a natural precision variant), a second engine such as SGLang with matched sampling parameters, and, where a provider declares fp8, an fp8 run. For each battery and member, fix the threshold at the 1−α quantile of a calibration split, check it on a second split, and apply the Clopper–Pearson upper bound for count statistics. A provider is rejected on a battery only if it is rejected against every member of H. Combine batteries with Holm, running each on disjoint queries so the p-values stay independent, and use an e-process for continuous monitoring. Recalibrate whenever the budget changes. Report three verdicts, not two: **consistent** with some member of H, **inconsistent with the declared configuration** (outside H but not clearly another model, which is where fp8 and kernel differences land), and **different model** (beyond the impostor band on T1 or T4, set from impostor distances such as the One Token 5th percentile). State in every verdict that 8-bit at 7B isn't ruled out unless the AWQ and fp8 power runs show otherwise.

### Ledger: commit the configuration, the null and the thresholds before the audit

For each reference run the ledger should carry the per-file weight manifest and HF commit, precision, engine and version, kernel flags (batch-invariant on or off), GPU model and count, TP degree, the chat template hash plus the rendered prompt-token count for each T0 conversation, BOS handling, every sampling parameter including repetition penalty and max tokens, the pinned date string for templates that embed one, and the system prompt (empty or not). For each battery it should carry the salted commitment to the prompt pool before use and the reveal after, sample hashes, and the honest-set membership with the measured pairwise statistics. Before any provider audit, commit α, the thresholds, the budget and the H members, so nobody can claim the cutoff was tuned after seeing the result; K2VV, OpenRouter and openbenchmarks can't show that today. Per audit, record the endpoint, provider-declared precision and region, routing pins (such as OpenRouter's `allow_fallbacks:false`), returned prompt-token counts, timestamps, the battery and harness versions, and the three-way verdict with power from the AWQ positive control. Leave room for provider-supplied claims (a TEE quote, a CommitLLM-style deployment commitment, a signed response envelope) so a later reader can join "attested image H at time T" to "reference R matched at time T".

## Conclusion

The field has quietly redefined the question. "Is this the model it claims?" has a reliable black-box answer only at the level of model identity and 4-bit precision; below that, the honest spread between real deployments is the signal, and every published verifier that looks closely enough ends up measuring kernels and templates. That makes the honest null, not the test, the scarce artifact. Whoever measures and publishes the band of honest variation for a model, before audits rather than after, sets the terms on which every later accusation is judged.

That is also the case for SPOT's ledger over a stronger test. Attestation and commit schemes will keep improving, but they're bilateral and opt-in, and the industry verifiers that exist publish editable tables. A Bitcoin-anchored record of the configuration, the sealed battery, the honest set and the thresholds is the only thing in this landscape that lets a third party check, months later, that the reference didn't move. The open risk is the one the evidence can't remove: an fp8 copy at 7B will pass SPOT's batteries, so the honest wording of a pass is "consistent with the declared model at the measured power", and SPOT's AWQ and fp8 runs are what turn that phrase into a number.
